# Historical Incident Patterns — Seattle / King County
## Category: Historical | Audience: 911 Dispatcher | Region: Seattle, WA

---

## Purpose
These patterns represent common incident types and optimal dispatch decisions for the Seattle area. Use as reference for recognizing scenario types quickly.

---

## Pattern 1 — Downtown Opioid Overdose (Most Common Single-Type Call)

**Typical call:** "There's a guy on the sidewalk, he's not moving, I can't wake him up. He might have OD'd."

**Key signals:** unresponsive, not moving, downtown / Capitol Hill / Belltown location, possibly blue or pale
**Dispatch:** 1× ALS ambulance, Priority 1
**Hospital:** Harborview Medical Center (nearest Level 1 to downtown, psychiatric/substance services)
**Notes:** Ask if caller or bystander has naloxone. High volume in Pioneer Square, 3rd Ave corridor, Capitol Hill.
**Outcome reference:** ALS median response 5 min downtown. Naloxone administered on-scene in ~60% of cases. Most patients transported to Harborview ED.

---

## Pattern 2 — I-5 / I-90 Multi-Vehicle MVC

**Typical call:** "There's been a bad crash on the freeway, I can see at least three cars, one is rolled over."

**Key signals:** freeway location, rollover, multiple vehicles, unknown number of injuries
**Dispatch:** 2× ALS ambulance + 2× fire engines + 2× police (WSP on interstate), Priority 1
**Hospital:** Harborview Medical Center for trauma; UW Medical Center as overflow
**Notes:** Rollover and ejection = high-energy mechanism, always Harborview. Notify WSDOT traffic management for freeway closures. WSP handles freeway jurisdiction.
**Common complications:** 2nd and 3rd vehicles discovered as additional units arrive. Pre-position staging.

---

## Pattern 3 — Cardiac Arrest (Residential)

**Typical call:** "My husband collapsed, he's not breathing, I think it's his heart."

**Key signals:** collapse, not breathing, history cardiac / elderly, panicked spouse on line
**Dispatch:** 2× ALS + 1× fire engine, Priority 1
**Hospital:** Harborview (cardiac cath if ROSC). Note "last known well" time.
**Notes:** Seattle has high bystander CPR rates — King County Medic One program. Confirm CPR in progress. Nearest King County Medic One station determines response time. Suburban response times 7–10 min.
**Outcome reference:** King County cardiac arrest survival with bystander CPR ~20%. Every minute without CPR decreases survival ~10%.

---

## Pattern 4 — Downtown Structure Fire (SoDo / Georgetown Industrial)

**Typical call:** "There's smoke pouring out of a building on Airport Way, looks like it could be a warehouse."

**Key signals:** industrial area, warehouse / large structure, visible smoke, SoDo / Georgetown / SODO corridor
**Dispatch:** 3× fire engines + 1× ladder truck + 1× ALS ambulance + battalion chief, Priority 1
**Notes:** SoDo and Georgetown have industrial buildings with unknown chemical storage. Default to hazmat awareness until fire crew can assess. Boeing facilities south of Georgetown — notify Boeing fire if adjacent. Seattle Fire Department SoDo stations: Station 28, Station 10.
**Hospital:** Harborview for any burn injuries.

---

## Pattern 5 — Puget Sound / Lake Washington Water Emergency

**Typical call:** "There's a kayaker in the water near Alki Beach, he capsized, I can see him waving but he's going under."

**Key signals:** water location, person in water, possible hypothermia (water temp 50–55°F year-round in Puget Sound)
**Dispatch:** Fire marine unit + ALS ambulance (at shore), Priority 1
**Also notify:** US Coast Guard Sector Puget Sound (for Puget Sound / Elliott Bay incidents)
**Hospital:** Harborview (hypothermia / trauma). Note: Puget Sound water is 50–55°F — hypothermia onset within 30 minutes even in summer.
**Key locations:** Alki Beach (Station 32 marine), Lake Union (Station 5), Eastlake, Portage Bay.

---

## Pattern 6 — Eastside Tech Campus Medical (Amazon / Microsoft)

**Typical call:** "Someone collapsed at our office in South Lake Union / Redmond, we think it's a seizure, there's security here."

**Key signals:** office building, corporate campus, security on scene, adult male (common demographic), seizure or cardiac
**Dispatch:**
- Seattle / SLU: 1× ALS, Priority 1 or 2 based on consciousness
- Redmond / Eastside: 1× ALS from Eastside, Overlake Medical Center for non-critical; Harborview for cardiac arrest
**Notes:** Large campuses (Amazon SLU, Microsoft Redmond) have on-site medical response teams. Confirm if AED used and shock delivered. Elevator access — ask security to meet unit at lobby.

---

## Pattern 7 — Chemical Incident at Port of Seattle / Terminal

**Typical call:** "There's a container leaking at the port, I can see a yellow cloud and workers are coughing."

**Key signals:** port location, yellow or white cloud, workers symptomatic, unknown substance
**Dispatch:** 2× hazmat units + 3× fire engines + 2× ALS ambulances, Priority 1
**Isolation:** 300 meters minimum until substance identified (assume chlorine-class hazard)
**Hospital:** Harborview for any symptomatic patients
**Also notify:** Port of Seattle Harbor Patrol, USCG, WA State Dept of Ecology Spill Response
**Notes:** Port of Seattle handles significant chemical freight. Common hazardous cargo: chlorine, ammonia refrigerants, industrial solvents. Workers in IDLH environments — decon before hospital transport.

---

## Pattern 8 — Homeless Encampment Fire (Under Bridge / Greenspace)

**Typical call:** "There's a tent on fire under the Aurora Bridge, I think someone's inside."

**Key signals:** encampment, tent fire, possible occupant, bridge / greenspace, access constraints
**Dispatch:** 1× fire engine + 1× ALS ambulance, Priority 1
**Notes:** Common locations: Aurora Bridge (N 36th St), I-5 underpasses, Jungle (South Beacon Hill). Limited vehicle access — foot approach may be needed. High probability of propane canisters / accelerant materials in encampments.
**Hospital:** Harborview for burn injuries. Frequently involves patients with no ID — note physical description in record.

## Common Dispatch Errors to Avoid (Seattle-Specific)

| Error | Correct Action |
|-------|----------------|
| Sending BLS to unresponsive downtown patient — likely opioid | Always ALS for unresponsive — do not downgrade based on assumed cause |
| Sending to Swedish/Virginia Mason for major trauma | Major trauma always to Harborview — it's the only Level 1 |
| Forgetting WSDOT/WSP notification for freeway incidents | WSP jurisdiction on I-5, I-90, SR-99, SR-520 — notify immediately |
| Routing pediatric patient to adult trauma center | Seattle Children's for all pediatric if time allows; Harborview if critical and closer |
| Forgetting Coast Guard for Puget Sound water calls | USCG Sector Puget Sound: (206) 217-6001 |
