# Medical Dispatch Guide — Dispatcher Reference
## Category: Medical | Audience: 911 Dispatcher | Region: Seattle / King County

---

## ALS vs BLS Dispatch Criteria

### Always Dispatch ALS (Advanced Life Support — Paramedic Unit)
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing / shortness of breath
- Unconscious or unresponsive patient
- Suspected stroke (facial droop, arm weakness, slurred speech)
- Seizure — active or postictal
- Suspected overdose — opioid or unknown substance
- Cardiac arrest (CPR in progress)
- Severe allergic reaction / anaphylaxis (throat swelling, can't breathe)
- Major trauma: GSW, stabbing, MVC with injuries, fall from significant height
- Diabetic emergency — altered mental status or unresponsive
- Imminent childbirth or obstetric complication

### BLS Acceptable (Basic Life Support — EMT Unit)
- Minor injuries — lacerations, sprains, minor fractures
- Patient sick but alert and speaking normally
- Vomiting / nausea without altered consciousness
- Fainting — patient now awake and oriented
- Minor allergic reaction — hives only, no breathing difficulty

### Upgrade to Two ALS Units When
- Cardiac arrest confirmed
- Pediatric cardiac or respiratory arrest
- MCI with multiple patients
- Entrapment requiring fire rescue alongside

## Hospital Selection — Seattle / King County

### Major Trauma (penetrating, high-energy MVC, fall from height)
→ **Harborview Medical Center** — only Level 1 trauma in Pacific Northwest
→ If Harborview at capacity: **UW Medical Center** (Level 2)

### Burns (any significant burns)
→ **Harborview Medical Center** — only burn center in Washington State
→ No substitute in region — always Harborview for burns

### Cardiac Emergency (chest pain, suspected STEMI)
→ **Harborview** (cardiac_cath 24/7)
→ **Swedish First Hill** (strong cardiac program, closer to some neighborhoods)
→ **UW Medical Center** (cardiac_cath available)

### Stroke
→ Nearest hospital with stroke_team — time is the priority
→ Harborview, UW, or Swedish First Hill all have 24/7 stroke teams

### Pediatric Emergency (patient under 18)
→ **Seattle Children's Hospital** — pediatric Level 1 trauma
→ Exception: if patient too critical to survive 15-minute drive, go to Harborview first

### Psychiatric Emergency
→ **Harborview Medical Center** — psychiatric emergency department
→ **UW Medical Center** — psychiatric inpatient

### Obstetric / High-Risk Delivery
→ **UW Medical Center** or **Swedish First Hill** (both have Level 3+ NICU)

### East King County Calls (Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland, Issaquah)
→ **Overlake Medical Center** for non-major trauma
→ **Harborview** for Level 1 trauma needs — 20-minute drive from Bellevue

### Spinal Cord Injury
→ **Harborview Medical Center** — regional spinal cord center

## Pre-Arrival Instructions — What to Tell the Caller

### Patient unconscious / not breathing
- "Stay calm. Units are on the way."
- "Is the patient breathing normally?"
- Keep caller on the line — CPR guidance via protocol card if trained dispatcher

### Patient bleeding
- "Apply firm, steady pressure with a cloth or towel and don't remove it."
- "Keep them still."

### Patient with chest pain
- "Have them sit or lie in whatever position is most comfortable."
- "Don't give them food or drink."
- "Loosen any tight clothing."

### Suspected overdose (unresponsive)
- "Do you have naloxone (Narcan) available?"
- "Help is on the way. If they're breathing, roll them onto their side."

### Suspected stroke
- "Do not give them anything to eat or drink."
- "Note what time the symptoms started — I need that exact time."
- "Keep them calm and still."

## Priority Levels

| Priority | Response Mode | When |
|----------|--------------|------|
| Priority 1 — IMMEDIATE | Lights and siren | Life threat — cardiac, stroke, arrest, severe trauma |
| Priority 2 — URGENT | Lights and siren | Serious but currently stable |
| Priority 3 — NON-URGENT | No lights/siren | Minor injury, patient alert and stable |

---

## Key Questions for Any Medical Call

1. **"What is the exact address?"** — always first
2. **"What happened? What is wrong?"**
3. **"How old is the patient? Male or female?"**
4. **"Is the patient conscious and breathing?"** — determines ALS vs BLS
5. **"Is anyone doing CPR?"** — if yes, confirm ALS + Priority 1 immediately
6. **"Is the scene safe? Any weapons or hazards present?"**

## Seattle-Specific Notes

### Fentanyl / Opioid Overdose — High Volume
Seattle EMS responds to a high volume of opioid overdose calls. King County Medic One units carry naloxone. Dispatchers should:
- Ask about naloxone availability at scene
- Note if caller reports patient is blue or not breathing — Priority 1 immediately
- Common locations: downtown Seattle, Capitol Hill, Belltown, SoDo

### Hypothermia (Unsheltered Population)
Seattle has a significant unsheltered population, especially during winter months. Hypothermia calls:
- ALS if altered mental status or unresponsive
- BLS if alert and ambulatory — transport to nearest ER
- Dispatch fire for rescue assist if patient in difficult access area

### Water Rescue — Puget Sound, Lake Washington, Lake Union
- Dispatch: fire marine unit + ALS
- Also notify US Coast Guard if in Puget Sound waters
- Key marine stations: Station 5 (Lake Union), Station 10 (West Seattle)
