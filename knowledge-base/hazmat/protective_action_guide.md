# Hazmat Protective Action Guide — Dispatcher Reference
## Category: Hazmat | Source: FEMA ERG 2024 (Dispatcher Edition) | Audience: 911 Dispatcher

---

## What a Dispatcher Does in a Hazmat Incident
1. Get the exact location — building, street corner, nearest cross street
2. Identify the substance if the caller knows it
3. Dispatch hazmat team + fire engine + ALS unit
4. Give protective action distance to callers near the scene
5. Alert police to establish perimeter at the isolation distance
6. Do NOT advise caller to approach or investigate the substance

---

## Units to Dispatch — Hazmat Incidents

| Incident Type | Primary Units | Add-On |
|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Chemical spill (known) | Hazmat unit + Fire engine | ALS if injuries |
| Chemical spill (unknown) | Hazmat unit + 2× Fire engines | ALS + Police perimeter |
| Gas leak — natural gas | Fire engine + Gas utility company | ALS if injuries, Police perimeter |
| Gas leak — industrial chemical | Hazmat unit + 2× Fire engines | ALS + Police + Decon unit |
| Fuel spill (vehicle) | Fire engine | ALS if injuries |
| Carbon monoxide suspected | Fire engine (CO detector) | ALS if symptoms reported |
| Smoke/fumes, unknown source | Fire engine + Hazmat | ALS |
| Explosion with chemical concern | Multiple fire engines + Hazmat | ALS, Police, notify hospital |

## Common Chemicals — Isolation and Protective Action Distances

### Chlorine (ERG Guide 124)
- **Recognition:** Yellow-green gas, pungent bleach-like smell, visible cloud
- **Small spill (≤55 gal / single cylinder):**
  - Isolation radius: 300 meters (1,000 feet) all directions
  - Downwind protective action: 1.1 km (0.7 miles) day / 3.7 km (2.3 miles) night
- **Large spill:**
  - Isolation radius: 600 meters (2,000 feet) all directions
  - Downwind protective action: 3.5 km (2.2 miles) day / 8.4 km (5.2 miles) night
- **Tell caller:** Evacuate building immediately, move upwind and uphill, do not re-enter

### Ammonia — Anhydrous (ERG Guide 125)
- **Recognition:** Colorless gas, sharp choking smell, white vapor cloud
- **Small spill:**
  - Isolation radius: 90 meters (300 feet)
  - Downwind protective action: 0.5 km (0.3 miles) day / 1.5 km (0.9 miles) night
- **Large spill:**
  - Isolation radius: 300 meters (1,000 feet)
  - Downwind protective action: 2.5 km (1.6 miles) day / 7.2 km (4.5 miles) night
- **Tell caller:** Evacuate immediately, move to fresh air crosswind or upwind

### Carbon Monoxide (ERG Guide 119)
- **Recognition:** Colorless, odorless — NOT detectable by smell; symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea
- **Action:** Evacuate building immediately; do NOT re-enter
- **Isolation radius:** 100 meters (330 feet)
- **Note:** Building must be ventilated before re-entry — fire crew clears with CO meter

### Natural Gas / Methane (ERG Guide 115)
- **Recognition:** Rotten egg smell (odorant added), hissing sound near gas lines
- **Small leak — indoors:**
  - Do NOT use switches, phones, or create any spark inside
  - Evacuate building immediately
  - Call gas utility company
  - Isolation: 100 meters (330 feet)
- **Large rupture / outdoor pipeline:**
  - Isolation: 300 meters (1,000 feet)
  - Evacuate structures in downwind direction up to 1 km

### Gasoline / Fuel Spill (ERG Guide 128)
- **Small vehicle spill:**
  - Isolation: 50 meters (165 feet)
  - No ignition sources — engines off, no smoking
- **Large spill (tank truck, pipeline):**
  - Isolation: 300 meters (1,000 feet)
  - Downwind protective action: 0.5 km (0.3 miles)
  - Police perimeter required

### Unknown Substance / White Powder (ERG Guide 111 — initial)
- **Isolation:** 100 meters (330 feet) minimum until identified
- **Do not touch, inhale, or disturb the substance**
- Notify hazmat team immediately — possible biological/chemical agent
- Preserve scene; get names of anyone who touched the substance

## Evacuation vs Shelter-In-Place

### When to Advise Evacuation
- Fire or explosion risk
- Toxic gas that spreads quickly (chlorine, ammonia)
- Caller can safely exit within the isolation zone before exposure
- Wind direction confirmed — upwind route safe

### When to Advise Shelter-In-Place
- Evacuation would expose people to the hazard (dense gas cloud)
- Indoor shelter is sealed enough to reduce exposure
- Short-duration release expected (e.g., vehicle spill being controlled)
- **Instructions to caller:** "Close all windows and doors. Turn off HVAC. Seal gaps with wet towels. Stay on this line."

## Caller Instructions by Scenario

### Caller is near a chemical spill — outside
"Move away from the area immediately. Go upwind — walk away from the direction the wind is blowing toward. Do not go back toward the spill. How far are you now?"

### Caller smells gas indoors
"Everyone needs to leave the building right now. Do not use any light switches, appliances, or anything electrical. Leave doors open as you exit. Once outside, move away from the building and call us back."

### Caller reports someone collapsed near fumes
"Do not enter the area to help them. Emergency units with the right protective equipment are on the way. Stay at a safe distance upwind and keep this line open."

## Key Questions for Hazmat Calls

1. **"What is the exact address and which building?"**
2. **"What substance is involved — is there a placard or label?"**
3. **"Is the substance liquid, gas, or solid?"**
4. **"How large is the spill / release?"** (container size, visible cloud extent)
5. **"Which direction is the wind blowing?"** (upwind = safe direction)
6. **"Are there any injuries? Is anyone having trouble breathing?"**
7. **"Are there bystanders near the scene?"** (helps set perimeter size)
